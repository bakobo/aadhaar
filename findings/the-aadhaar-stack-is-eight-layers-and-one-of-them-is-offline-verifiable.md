# The Aadhaar stack is eight distinct things, and exactly one of them a stranger can verify

**Questions:** Q9 — what does India actually issue? Q14 — is the data model open, is the presentation protocol completable by a third party, is the trust anchor obtainable?
**Corpus:** `corpus-acts/` and `corpus-delegated/` at 2026-09-16. The operative instrument is the Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Regulations, 2021, as amended to 9 December 2025.
**Answer, short:** "Aadhaar" names an identifier, a rotating pseudonym, a per-verifier pseudonym, three signed documents, an online yes/no service and a federation gateway, and collapsing them produces nonsense. Only the **Secure QR** is verifiable by a party with no relationship to UIDAI. And since 9 December 2025 Indian delegated legislation contains a defined term, **"Aadhaar Verifiable Credential"**, shareable "in full or part" — which is selective disclosure, in a regulation, months after the landscape survey recorded that India ships no standards-based verifiable credentials outside pilots.

Every quotation below is verbatim from the corpus and reproducible with `tools/check-quotes.py`.

## 1. The layers, each with the provision that constitutes it

| Layer | What it is | Signed by | Verifiable offline by a stranger? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aadhaar number | s. 3, a number issued on enrolment | nothing | No — an identifier, not a credential |
| Virtual Identifier | Reg. 2(od) | nothing | No |
| UID Token | Reg. 2(oc) | n/a | n/a — a per-verifier pseudonym |
| Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC | Reg. 2(bb) | UIDAI | In principle |
| **Aadhaar Secure QR Code** | Reg. 2(bc) | UIDAI | **Yes** |
| Aadhaar PVC Card | Reg. 2(bd) | UIDAI | as the QR it carries |
| **Aadhaar Verifiable Credential** | Reg. 2(be), inserted 9-12-2025 | UIDAI | No — registered OVSEs only |
| Online authentication | s. 2(c), Reg. 3 | n/a | No — call-home by construction |

The two pseudonyms are worth stating in the regulation's own words, because they are the part of this architecture that the rest of the world arrived at later. `corpus-delegated/AOV-REGS-2021`:

> (oc) “UID Token” means a 72-character alphanumeric string generated by the Authority mapped to the Aadhaar number and specific to a requesting entity; (od) “Virtual Identifier” means an interchangeable 16-digit random number mapped with the Aadhaar number of the Aadhaar number holder;

A UID Token is a **pairwise pseudonym**: different for every relying party, so two verifiers cannot collude by comparing identifiers. India shipped that years before the EU wallet debate made it a talking point.

## 2. The Secure QR is the whole of the offline story

> (bc) “Aadhaar Secure QR Code” means a quick response code generated by the Authority which contains digitally signed data like last 4 digits of Aadhaar number, demographic data like name, address, gender, and date of birth, and photograph of the Aadhaar number holder etc.;

Three properties make it the only member of this stack a stranger can use. It is **generated and signed by the Authority**, so its authenticity does not depend on the presenter. It is **carried on the PVC card and in e-Aadhaar**, so the holder presents it without a network. And its signature is an ordinary Indian PKI signature — see the trust-anchor finding beside this one — so verifying it needs no relationship with UIDAI at all.

Note what the definition does *not* contain: no algorithm, no encoding, no format. That is the shape of the whole instrument. Over the stored text of the 2021 Regulations, `RSA`, `algorithm`, `public key`, `X.509` and `certificate` each occur zero times, against controls of `shall` at 154 and `Authority` at 234 — and Reg. 3A ends every list of offline verification types with "as per the specifications given by the Authority from time to time". **The law constitutes the artefact by naming its parts and delegates every parameter.** So any claim about the Indian wire format is a claim about a UIDAI specification and never about Indian law, and this corpus cannot support one.

## 3. The Aadhaar Verifiable Credential, and the date on it

Inserted into Reg. 2 by notification No. HQ-30011/5/2025-AU-HO of **9 December 2025**:

> (be) “Aadhaar Verifiable Credential” means a digitally signed document issued by the Authority to the Aadhaar number holder which may contain last 4 digits of Aadhaar number, demographic data, like, name, address, gender, date of birth, and photograph of Aadhaar number holder, and such other information as may be specified by the Authority, **which may be shared by Aadhaar number holder in full or part with an OVSE in the manner specified by the Authority**, for verifying the demographic information or photograph of the Aadhaar number holder;

and Reg. 3A was substituted at the same time to make it a service:

> 3A. Types of Offline Verification.—1. There shall be following types of Offline Verification services provided by the Authority, namely- (i) QR Code verification, (ii) Aadhaar Paperless Offline e-KYC verification, 12(ii)(a) Aadhaar Verifiable Credential verification, (iii) e-Aadhaar verification, (iv) Offline Paper based verification, and (v) Any other type of Offline verification introduced by the Authority from time to time.

Four things are true of that at once, and they pull in different directions.

**It is holder-presented and selectively disclosable, in the text of a regulation.** "Shared by Aadhaar number holder in full or part" is selective disclosure without the phrase. A state has put the holder in the middle of the flow, by instrument, and given them a choice about how much to reveal.

**It is not offline with respect to UIDAI.** Reg. 3A calls it offline verification, and Reg. 13A confines it to registered OVSEs who verify "through Aadhaar Application". Offline here means *not against the CIDR*; the Authority is still in the loop. The Secure QR and the AVC are on opposite sides of that line and the regulation calls both of them offline verification, which is a trap for anyone reading the list rather than the definitions.

**Whether it is a W3C Verifiable Credential is not a question Indian law answers.** The regulation names a thing and delegates its format. Nothing in this corpus says the letters "W3C", and a finding that read the name as a standards claim would be inventing one.

**It needs a date, because it changes what was true.** The landscape survey behind this programme records that India ships no standards-based verifiable credentials outside pilots. As of 9 December 2025 that sentence needs amending: **India has a credential called a Verifiable Credential defined in its delegated legislation, holder-shared and selectively disclosable.** What it is made of remains unpublished.

## 4. Q14, answered in its three parts, because the parts disagree

**Is the data model open?** For the Secure QR: not from the law, and UIDAI's own specification pages 404 from this box with positive controls firing (`sources/registry.md`). Third-party reader implementations agree on a layout, which is evidence of a *convention* and not of a published model. For the AVC: no specification was found at all. So — **no, and the reason is delegation rather than secrecy.**

**Is the presentation protocol completable by a third party?** Two opposite answers, and collapsing them is the error this finding exists to prevent. For the **AVC, no**: Reg. 13A confines it to registered OVSEs. For the **Secure QR, yes**: nothing in the Act or the Regulations conditions scanning a QR code and checking a signature on any relationship with UIDAI, and s. 8A(4) — which is the provision that *restricts* an offline verification-seeking entity — prohibits collecting the Aadhaar number and subjecting the holder to authentication, neither of which reading a signed QR involves.

**Is the trust anchor obtainable?** Yes. That is the subject of the finding beside this one, and it is the first yes in this programme.

## What this finding does not establish

- The Secure QR's byte layout, compression, or signature padding. Not in the corpus, not in the law, and not asserted here.
- Whether the Paperless Offline e-KYC XML really signs with RSA-SHA1, as third-party accounts say. If true it matters, and no primary source for it was reachable.
- Anything about DigiLocker's issuer API, whose specification CDN refuses this egress with a CloudFront 403 while the portal it belongs to serves 200.
